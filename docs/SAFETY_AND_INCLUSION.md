# Safety, inclusion, and cultural respect

These are product requirements, not optional tone guidance.

## 1. Ask, do not infer

Wearwell must not infer from a name, image, voice, location, clothing, or accent:

- nationality, ethnicity, culture, religion, or community membership;
- gender or gender expression;
- disability or medical condition;
- body type, attractiveness, age-based taste, or skin-tone “season”;
- income or budget.

Users can state the needs that matter for the outfit. Store those needs as editable constraints, not identity labels.

## 2. Language rules

Avoid:

- “flattering,” “hide,” “fix,” “slimming,” or “too old/young”;
- claims that clothing reveals character;
- claims that clothing guarantees confidence, intelligence, or professionalism;
- universal rules such as “this colour is wrong for your skin tone”;
- describing cultural clothing as “tribal,” “ethnic,” “exotic,” or costume;
- presenting one region's workplace norms as universal.

Prefer:

- “If you want a longer visual line, this jacket length is one option.”
- “This is a convention that varies by workplace.”
- “All colours are available to you; this suggestion follows the colours you said you enjoy.”
- “This garment has cultural context. If you are attending as a guest, ask the host what is appropriate and buy from a maker where possible.”

## 3. Hard constraint priority

The following override style novelty:

1. safety and PPE;
2. employer/school uniform requirements;
3. user-stated coverage or religious requirements;
4. user-stated mobility and sensory requirements;
5. weather and activity practicality;
6. comfort and fit;
7. preference, formality, novelty, and trend.

The product must not give medical, legal, or workplace safety advice beyond clearly sourced general guidance. When safety is specific to a workplace or activity, direct the user to the responsible authority.

## 4. Cultural context

When a garment or event is culturally or religiously specific:

- ask what the host, family, community, or venue expects;
- describe the garment's status carefully: everyday, festive, ceremonial, religious, or fashion;
- name uncertainty and internal diversity;
- avoid sacred, royal, clan-restricted, or community-owned items as generic styling props;
- recommend buying from makers or credited artisans where relevant;
- never treat one source or one person as representative of a whole culture.

The assistant should not treat Western clothing or workplace conventions as neutral defaults.

## 5. Accessibility and comfort

Ask whether the outfit must allow or avoid something:

- sitting, standing, walking, bending, or reaching;
- closures that are difficult to operate;
- tight waistbands or scratchy seams;
- heavy layers, slippery soles, or loose cords;
- heat, sweat, rain, or sensory triggers.

Offer adaptive features only when relevant to a stated need: elastic or adjustable waists, larger pulls, magnetic or hook-and-loop closures, wrap/open styles, stretch, soft seams, tagless construction, or stable footwear.

Do not use disability labels as a prerequisite for providing these options.

## 6. Evidence and uncertainty

The research supplied for this product distinguishes:

- E1: scholarly, museum, university, or peer-reviewed material retrieved;
- E2: institutional or secondary material;
- E3: design synthesis that still needs verification;
- E4: commercial or individual opinion.

The app should never surface E3/E4 material as scientific fact. The knowledge base should store claim type, scope, source, evidence strength, limitation, and review status.

## 7. Privacy

- Voice transcripts are opt-in to retain.
- Wardrobe images are private by default.
- Users can delete wardrobe data, images, feedback, and account data.
- Location is used only with permission and should not be displayed back unnecessarily.
- No data is shared with third parties beyond the providers necessary for the requested feature and disclosed in product settings.

## 8. Evaluation gates

Before pilot:

- 100% hard-constraint adherence on the golden set;
- zero body-judgement and stereotype flags in reviewed outputs;
- no cultural-costuming output in ceremony scenarios;
- explanations include the required fields;
- recommendations remain usable across size, age, budget, mobility, sensory, modesty, and regional-context cases;
- matched-pair tests change results only when stated constraints differ.
